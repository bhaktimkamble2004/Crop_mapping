01 • Project overview

What was investigated?

This project investigates whether multi-temporal Sentinel-1 Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) data can distinguish broad agricultural land conditions in a selected part of Baramati. The final classes are Active Cropland, Fallow Agricultural Field and Non-Cropland.

Individual crop species were not used as final classes because reliable field-level crop labels were unavailable. Sentinel-2 true-colour imagery and NDVI were used only as visual support while preparing reference samples.

Research question

How effectively can multi-temporal Sentinel-1 SAR data and Random Forest classification distinguish active cropland, fallow agricultural fields and non-cropland in the selected Baramati study area?

02 • Data

Why Sentinel-1 SAR?

All-weather observation

SAR can collect information through most cloud conditions, making it useful during cloudy agricultural seasons.

VV and VH

VV and VH backscatter respond differently to surface and vegetation structure. Their temporal behaviour was used as classification information.

Multi-temporal approach

Multiple observations were used so the model could capture changes in agricultural surfaces rather than depend on one image.

Dataset: Sentinel-1 GRD Mode: IW Polarisation: VV + VH Orbit: Descending, relative orbit 136 Season analysed: 2026

08 • Literature

Mandatory paper review

Veloso et al. (2017)

Temporal crop behaviour using Sentinel-1 and optical information; supported the use of multi-temporal VV/VH observations.

Bargiel (2017)

Radar time series and crop phenology; reinforced the value of observations across the growing season.

Xu et al. (2019)

Temporal SAR information for crop mapping; supported analysis of temporal patterns rather than a single acquisition.

Mishra et al. (2022)

Indian agricultural application using Sentinel-1 and machine learning; relevant to the regional classification approach.

Rege et al. (2022)

Optical and radar mapping of cashew in the Western Ghats; highlighted both the usefulness and limitations of radar-based agricultural mapping.

09 • Interpretation

Limitations and conclusion

Key limitations

  • Reference labels were interpreted mainly from satellite imagery rather than field survey observations.
  • Fallow agricultural land can resemble bare or developed non-cropland in SAR data.
  • The available 2026 observations did not represent a completely finished agricultural season.
  • Individual crop species were not mapped because verified field-level crop labels were unavailable.
  • The initial pixel classification contained salt-and-pepper noise, so cautious post-processing was applied.

Conclusion

Multi-temporal Sentinel-1 SAR and Random Forest classified the study area into three broad agricultural land classes with an Overall Accuracy of 79.89% and Kappa of 0.647. Active Cropland was identified most successfully, while Fallow Agricultural Field showed the greatest confusion.
